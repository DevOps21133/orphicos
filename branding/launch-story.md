# OrphicOS — Launch Narrative (solo founder, early access)

Principle: no growth hacks, no fake urgency, no invented numbers. Each stage tells one true story to one audience. The demo is the marketing; everything else points at it.

## The sequence

### 1. The demo video (the foundation — nothing launches before this)

One unbroken screen recording of the money task, spoken: "Go through the PDFs in the invoices folder, put each vendor and total into a new Excel sheet, sum the column, then write a short summary in Notepad." No cuts inside the run, no sped-up footage without an on-screen label, real latency visible. The OrphicOS window with wordmark, live log, and STOP button stays in frame — the safety UI *is* the demo, not a disclaimer.

Story angle: "I said one sentence and watched my PC do fifteen minutes of work across three applications." The honesty is the hook: unedited, latency and all, kill switch visible. In a field of stitched-together agent demos, an uncut run is the differentiator.

Also record a 30–45s cut (the command, two mid-run moments, the finished spreadsheet) for embeds and threads.

### 2. Landing page + early access list

One page: wordmark, tagline ("the machine works. You don't."), the demo video, three short sections mapping the messaging pillars (say it → it happens; you stay at the helm; honest data handling — approved claim verbatim), and one email field: "Request early access."

Story angle: seriousness. Charcoal/bone restraint, no hype adjectives, no fake counters, no "join 10,000 others." The visual identity carries the "serious company" signal; the copy just describes the product. State plainly: early access, Windows 11, invite waves as capacity allows — a solo builder being straightforward reads as confidence.

### 3. Niche communities (in order)

Post as a person, not a brand. Each post is written for that community, discloses "I built this," and leads with the demo. Follow each community's self-promo rules; if a community forbids it, don't post.

- **Accessibility / RSI communities** (r/disability, r/RSI, a11y forums, Talon/Dragon user circles) — first, because need is highest and feedback is sharpest. Angle: "I'm building voice control that executes whole tasks, not just dictation — I want it to actually work for people who need it. What would you test first?" Ask, don't pitch. Their bug reports are the roadmap; say so.
- **Productivity / automation communities** (r/productivity, AutoHotkey and PowerToys circles) — angle: "the automation is the sentence itself — no script, no setup." These users will probe limits publicly; answer honestly, including what fails.
- **r/windows / Windows enthusiast spaces** — angle: the architecture story. Windows people respect mechanism: it reads the UI Automation tree — the same structure screen readers use — so it clicks the actual named button instead of guessing at pixels. Thin client on your PC, decisions on the OrphicOS brain. (Never the provider; if asked directly: "we use a third-party model on our servers under a zero-retention setup — which one is an internal detail we keep swappable.")

### 4. Build-in-public thread arc (X/Twitter + longer notes)

A continuing story, one honest chapter at a time — the solo-founder narrative is the moat no big company can copy.

Arc:
1. **The premise** — forty years of doing every click ourselves; command line → GUI → touch → the next step is stating the outcome. Ends on the tagline.
2. **The architecture** — thin client / hosted brain, tree-first perception, why voice never leaves the device. Diagrams, not adjectives.
3. **The safety model** — why the kill switch and approval gate exist; film the kill switch actually halting a run mid-task. Failure honesty is the content: "here's the run where it opened the wrong window, and here's what I changed."
4. **The demo drop** — the uncut video, with the story of how many takes reliability took (true number).
5. **Early access opens** — first outside users, real reactions (with permission), what broke in their hands.

Rules for the arc: real numbers or no numbers; show failures at roughly the rate they happen; never announce features that don't exist yet without "(building this next)"; every post survives the banned-claims list in messaging.md.

### What we don't do

No paid ads at this stage, no engagement-bait ("RT for access"), no astroturfed posts from alt accounts, no cold DMs, no fake scarcity, no leaderboard/referral gimmicks, no "we" implying a team that doesn't exist. One founder, one product, one uncut demo, told plainly. The compounding asset is trust.
